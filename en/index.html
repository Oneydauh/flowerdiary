Beginner guide

Getting started with manifestation

Understand the methods, then learn the app’s names. One simple entry is enough to start today.

1. What is manifestation?

Start by asking: what would a day feel like if my wish came true?

Manifestation refers to ideas and practices that seek to realize wishes through changes in beliefs, imagination and feelings. Common practices include affirmations, scripting and meditation. The Law of Attraction and Law of Assumption offer different explanations for how this might work.

For example, imagine an ordinary moment after starting a job you want: how you present your work or interact with colleagues. You can write it down and choose one practical preparation to make today. First, let’s explore the terms.

01 Law of Attraction Law of Attraction

A manifestation belief that thoughts and feelings attract corresponding experiences, often practiced through imagination, gratitude and affirmations.

Origins
Rhonda Byrne’s The Secret (2006) helped popularize this idea but was not its earliest source.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
In this view, experience the feeling of your wish fulfilled, then attend to everyday experiences and actions consistent with it.
Effects & evidence
The book’s language of thought “frequencies” belongs to its author’s explanatory framework; it is not an experimental finding in physics or psychology.

Resources: The Secret: official introduction

Search terms: Law of Attraction；The Secret；Rhonda Byrne

In FlowerDiary · Vision board, gratitude and scripting

These tools can support the practices. Law of Attraction is not a separate button in the app.

Vision board; or + → the relevant tool · View instructions

02 Law of Assumption Law of Assumption

Accept the wish as fulfilled in imagination, experiencing the corresponding identity, feelings and inner dialogue.

Origins
A central idea in Neville Goddard’s work, including The Power of Awareness.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Goddard proposes entering the state of a wish already fulfilled, rather than repeatedly thinking “I want”.
Effects & evidence
This is Goddard’s philosophy of consciousness. Describing its practices does not establish that imagination necessarily determines external events.

Resources: Archive of Neville Goddard’s writings

Search terms: Law of Assumption；Neville Goddard；The Power of Awareness

In FlowerDiary · New identity and scripting

Use identity descriptions and scenes from life for the practice. There is no app feature named Law of Assumption.

Vision board → current chapter summary; + → Scripting · View instructions

03 Self-concept Self-concept

Your understanding of your qualities, abilities, value and roles. Manifestation discussions often use it to mean “who I believe I am”.

Origins
A general concept in psychology, not a term exclusive to one manifestation method.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Notice the descriptions you repeatedly use about yourself, then choose perspectives and behaviors you want to practice.
Effects & evidence
Writing an identity statement does not mean every belief or real-world circumstance has changed.

Resources: APA: self-concept

Search terms: Self-concept；Self-image；Identity

In FlowerDiary · New identity

New identity is the self-description field for your current chapter. It captures only part of your self-concept.

Vision board → expand current chapter summary → tap the identity description · View instructions

04 Vision board Vision Board

A collection of pictures and words describing the life you hope to experience, used as a visual reminder of your goals.

Origins
A common format in goal planning and manifestation practice, usually a collage of images and text.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Use specific images to clarify what you want and remind yourself of your next steps.
Effects & evidence
Displaying a goal is different from accomplishing it. Pictures do not replace preparation or action.

Search terms: Vision Board；Visualization；Goal Imagery

In FlowerDiary · Vision board and goals

Each goal can contain text and up to ten images. Category folders and card stacks are ways the app displays them.

Vision board → Add goal · View instructions

05 Affirmations Affirmation

A deliberately chosen statement repeated to practice new self-talk. The term describes a type of statement, not a required number of readings or repetitions.

Origins
Louise Hay is one of the well-known authors associated with modern affirmation practice.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Hay proposes that choosing positive statements can retrain habitual thinking and speech by repeatedly encountering a self-description you hope to accept.
Effects & evidence
Stronger statements are not necessarily better. Research found that some participants with low self-esteem felt worse after repeating strongly positive self-statements.

Resources: Louise Hay: affirmations · Wood et al. (2009): original study

Search terms: Positive Affirmations；Louise Hay；Positive Self-statements

In FlowerDiary · Goal affirm, 369 writing and Create SUB

Affirm lets you read a statement and confirm it; 369 schedules writing; Create SUB turns statements into audio. They are not synonyms.

Vision board → goal → … → affirm; or + → 369 writing / Create SUB · View instructions

06 Scripting Scripting

Write as the author of your life, describing scenes, dialogue and feelings as if your wish has already come true.

Origins
Esther and Jerry Hicks list Scripting as Process 9 in Ask and It Is Given (2004), a traceable representative source.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
In the Hicks approach, writing helps you experience fulfillment in advance and align your feelings with the life you want.
Effects & evidence
This is an imagination and writing practice. Adding detail does not mean events will unfold exactly as written.

Resources: Book contents and bibliographic details

Search terms: Manifestation Scripting；Ask and It Is Given；Process 9；Esther Jerry Hicks

In FlowerDiary · Scripting

The app groups writing into dawn, morning, afternoon and night. These are prompts; writing just one section is fine.

+ → Scripting · View instructions

07 369 writing 369 Method

Choose an affirmation about your wish. A common practice is writing it three times in the morning, six at noon and nine in the evening—a schedule for repeated writing.

Origins
Popular in modern manifestation communities. Its original creator has not been reliably verified, and online attributions to Tesla lack reliable sourcing.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Use fixed repetitions across three parts of the day to keep returning to the same wish.
Effects & evidence
A schedule can support a habit. Claims that the numbers 3, 6 and 9 possess special manifestation powers lack reliable validation.

Resources: Checking the source of the Tesla quote

Search terms: 369 Method；369 Manifestation；369 Journaling

In FlowerDiary · 369 writing

Enter today’s affirmation at the top, then use three morning, six noon and nine evening lines. Complete this period fills blank lines. You still need to save after completing all periods.

+ → 369 writing · View instructions

08 Gratitude journaling Gratitude Journaling

Record something you appreciate and why it matters to you.

Origins
Gratitude practices appear in many traditions. Emmons and McCullough (2003) provide a representative study of gratitude journaling.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Revisit specific support and positive events, turning a general “thank you” into an experience you can return to.
Effects & evidence
These studies examine experiences such as subjective well-being, not whether gratitude writing fulfills a particular wish.

Resources: McCullough: original study bibliography

Search terms: Gratitude Journaling；Counting Blessings Versus Burdens；Emmons McCullough 2003

In FlowerDiary · Gratitude

Write something you appreciate and save it, with optional pictures. This is the app’s gratitude entry point.

+ → Gratitude · View instructions

09 Limiting beliefs Limiting Belief

In personal growth, fixed judgments that narrow choices or discourage action, such as “If I fail, I must be incapable”.

Origins
A common personal-growth term. It can be understood alongside core beliefs and rigid assumptions in cognitive behavioral theory, but the terms are not identical.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Describe the situation and judgment, examine the evidence, and explore a more balanced understanding and feasible action.
Effects & evidence
Recording a thought, changing an interpretation and completing a release practice are different actions.

Resources: Beck Institute: cognitive behavioral theory

Search terms: Limiting Beliefs；Core Beliefs；Cognitive Restructuring

In FlowerDiary · Limiting beliefs

Save the specific thought first. In that day’s entry, tap Release. Saving a belief does not automatically complete a release.

+ → Limiting beliefs · View instructions

10 Letting go / Sedona Method Letting Go / Sedona Method

Letting go broadly means reducing resistance to feelings. The Sedona Method is a particular self-help approach using questions to allow and explore releasing present feelings.

Origins
The Sedona Method traces its origins to Lester Levenson and has been taught and shared by Hale Dwoskin and others.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Allow the feeling, then use simple questions to explore loosening your hold on it. The aim is less tension and clearer thinking; repeat as needed.
Effects & evidence
Research on emotional acceptance can help explain the approach but does not directly validate a specific Sedona sequence.

Resources: Sedona: official history · Official question examples

Search terms: The Sedona Method；Lester Levenson；Hale Dwoskin；Letting Go

In FlowerDiary · Release practice

The cards draw on Sedona questions, in the order Allow → Would you? → Could you? → When? You can also use your own audio guidance.

+ → Release practice; or a belief entry for the day → Release · View instructions

11 SUB / subliminal audio Subliminal Audio

Manifestation communities often use SUB to mean quiet suggestions mixed with background audio. Strictly, subliminal refers to stimulation below the threshold of conscious perception.

Origins
Subliminal is a psychological concept. A community label on an audio track does not mean its stimuli have been experimentally verified as subliminal.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Listeners hope repeated suggestions can influence beliefs or self-evaluation without demanding attention. A common approach mixes quiet suggestions with background sound.
Effects & evidence
Not hearing speech clearly does not mean its perceptual threshold has been measured. Greenwald et al. (1991) found no claimed memory or self-esteem improvements from the self-help tapes tested in their double-blind study.

Resources: APA: definition of subliminal · Greenwald et al. (1991): double-blind study

Search terms: Subliminal Perception；Subliminal Self-help Audiotapes；Affirmation Audio

In FlowerDiary · Create SUB and Listen to SUB

The app creates affirmation audio with adjustable voice and background levels; it does not measure individual perceptual thresholds. Creating and listening have separate entry points.

+ → Create SUB; + → Listen to SUB · View instructions

12 Mindfulness meditation Mindfulness Meditation

Attend to your body, natural breathing, sounds and thoughts as they arise, returning when you notice your attention has wandered.

Origins
Mindfulness has roots in meditation traditions. Organizations such as the NHS and UCLA introduce modern foundational practices.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Start in a safe, comfortable position. Notice your body, then choose breathing or points of contact as an anchor. Open your eyes, adjust or stop whenever you need.
Effects & evidence
Public resources describe these principles but do not establish therapeutic effects for this app’s courses. Chakra names come from traditional beliefs, not proven medical mechanisms.

Resources: NHS: mindfulness · UCLA: introductory practices

Search terms: Mindfulness Meditation；Body Scan；Breath Awareness

In FlowerDiary · Meditation

For adults, with original Mandarin guidance lasting 3, 5, 12, 19, 25 or 45 minutes. The 12/19-minute sessions offer body themes. Read the notice before your first session; start with 3 or 5 minutes. Pause or stop anytime—there is no need to endure discomfort.

+ → Meditation → duration → Before you practice → Start · View instructions

3. Understanding names in the app

Affirmations are statements; 369 is a writing method; affirm is the app interaction for reading statements and confirming them.

Chapter

A collection of the identity, goals and journal entries for one stage of your life.

Starting a new chapter archives the previous one. Revisit archived chapters in Profile.

New identity

A self-description for this stage, such as “I am willing to express my needs”.

It captures part of your self-concept, not everything you know about yourself. Edit it by expanding the current chapter summary on the vision board.

affirm

An affirmation exercise in a goal’s detail menu: read a statement, then tap “yes!” three times before the next statement appears.

Path: Vision board → goal → … → affirm. Confirmation counts are not journal entry counts.

Statement library

A list for saving and managing practice statements within affirm.

Open it inside affirm to add, edit or delete affirmations. Statement library is the app’s name for this list.

Book of Answers

A tool that draws a random response from a built-in collection. Save the question and response to revisit them in your journal.

It offers prompts for reflection, not analysis or predictions. Saved questions and answers cannot be edited.

The Affirmations journal category

A journal category for affirmation-related entries, including saved 369 practices.

This is a content category. Tapping “yes!” in affirm does not create an affirmation journal entry.

4. Just four things to do today

Write an identity, add a goal, write a gratitude entry, then look back.

First: write your new identity

Where to start

First-use guide → Write your new identity

  1. Write a sentence about who you want to become in this stage of life.
  2. Save it to create your current chapter.

Second: add one goal

Where to start

Vision board → Add goal

  1. Choose a goal category, such as Career.
  2. Describe the goal, being specific about what you want.
  3. Add pictures if you like, then tap Save.

Third: write a gratitude entry

Where to start

+ → Gratitude

  1. Write about one small thing that happened and that you appreciate.
  2. Add why it matters to you, then tap Save.

Fourth: revisit your entry

Where to start

Bottom navigation: Journal → Today

  1. Find the gratitude entry you just saved and read it.
  2. Think of one small next step, such as organizing a page of your portfolio. Your first practice today is complete.

5. Recording feelings and signs

Keep gratitude, feelings and progress in separate categories to find them more easily later.

Mood journaling Mood Journaling

Record present emotions, feelings and their context so you can revisit changes over time.

Origins
A common journaling and self-observation practice, not exclusive to any manifestation theory.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Name your real feelings, then notice what happened and what you need.
Effects & evidence
You do not need to stay positive or turn uncomfortable feelings into a good mood.

Search terms: Mood Journaling；Emotion Awareness；Emotion Diary

In FlowerDiary · Feelings

Write an entry or simply select mood tags and save. You do not need to write a paragraph first.

+ → Feelings · View instructions

Where to start

+ → Feelings

  1. Write how you feel, or select one or more mood tags.
  2. Tap Save. If you do not feel like writing, tags alone can be saved.

Manifestation signs Manifestation Signs

A community term for events, coincidences or associations connected to a wish, sometimes called “responses from the universe”.

Origins
A community expression without a single professional standard of assessment.
Approach, evidence & resources
Approach
Record the event and its meaning to you, distinguishing what happened from how you interpret it.
Effects & evidence
A meaningful coincidence is not the same as an achieved outcome. Notice whether you are attending only to information that supports your wish.

Resources: APA: confirmation bias

Search terms: Manifestation Signs；Synchronicity；Confirmation Bias

In FlowerDiary · Signs appeared

Record related experiences. Linking a goal can increase its signs count, but does not automatically mark it manifested.

+ → Signs appeared · View instructions

Where to start

+ → Signs appeared

  1. Describe a related development or something that brought your wish to mind.
  2. Separate what happened from your interpretation, then save.

Gratitude, feelings and signs entries can each include up to nine pictures.

6. Other manifestation tools

Beginners do not need to complete a whole routine every day. Start by getting comfortable with recording.

  • To describe life after your wish comes true, use Scripting. Write by dawn, morning, afternoon and night, or just one section.
  • If you like a fixed writing schedule, try 369 writing: three morning, six noon and nine evening repetitions of an affirmation. Finish all three periods, then save today’s entry.
  • To explore recurring judgments about yourself, record a Limiting belief. After saving today’s entry, you can tap Release.
  • To explore allowing and letting go through questions, use the cards in Release practice, or import your own guided audio.
  • To listen to your affirmations, make them in Create SUB and open your audio library through Listen to SUB.
  • For a prompt to reflect on, draw a response in the Book of Answers. You can save it to your journal or leave it unsaved.

For a few quiet minutes, try meditation

Where to start

+ → Meditation → choose 3, 5, 12, 19, 25 or 45 minutes → Start meditation

Meditation is for adults. Try 3 or 5 minutes first and read the notice before starting. A 12-second preparation includes a gentle bowl cue. Follow body and natural breath awareness offline. Pause or stop anytime; do not push through discomfort or feel you must make up a session.

Bring practice back into everyday life

After imagining a satisfying interview, prepare your work, learn about the role and practice speaking. Practices can support attention and action but cannot guarantee external outcomes.

7. Frequently asked questions

Your writing does not need to be beautiful or lengthy every day.

What if I cannot think of anything to appreciate?

Start small: a warm meal, a rest or someone’s help. If gratitude writing does not feel right today, record your real feelings instead.

Will worry or doubt affect my practice?

You can still write. You do not need to be positive all the time or blame yourself for a thought. Describe the worry, then decide whether to rest, act or try releasing.

Can I edit earlier entries?

Ordinary entries from today in the current chapter can be edited or deleted. Earlier dates and archived chapters are read-only. Saved Book of Answers questions and responses cannot be edited.

What should I do tomorrow?

Write another gratitude entry or record your feelings. After a few days, use Calendar to choose a date or Garden to review by category. Find a way of writing you want to keep using.
