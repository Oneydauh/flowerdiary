Practice guide

Manifestation methods: meanings, differences and practice

Tried a few methods and wondering how they differ? Each section introduces the concept, its origins and approach, then explains how to use FlowerDiary. Read what you need; there is no need to try every method.

1. Self-concept and vision

When you have many goals but no clear starting point, distinguish an identity description from a specific goal.

Self-concept Self-concept

Your understanding of your qualities, abilities, value and roles. Manifestation discussions often use it to mean “who I believe I am”.

Origins
A general concept in psychology, not a term exclusive to one manifestation method.
Approach
Notice the descriptions you repeatedly use about yourself, then choose perspectives and behaviors you want to practice.
Effects & evidence
Writing an identity statement does not mean every belief or real-world circumstance has changed.

Resources: APA: self-concept

Search terms: Self-concept；Self-image；Identity

In FlowerDiary · New identity

New identity is the self-description field for your current chapter. It captures only part of your self-concept.

Where to start

Vision board → current chapter summary → identity description

  1. Expand the current chapter summary and tap the identity description.
  2. Write and save an identity for this stage, such as “I express my needs clearly and listen carefully to feedback”.

Vision board Vision Board

A collection of pictures and words describing the life you hope to experience, used as a visual reminder of your goals.

Origins
A common format in goal planning and manifestation practice, usually a collage of images and text.
Approach
Use specific images to clarify what you want and remind yourself of your next steps.
Effects & evidence
Displaying a goal is different from accomplishing it. Pictures do not replace preparation or action.

Search terms: Vision Board；Visualization；Goal Imagery

In FlowerDiary · Vision board and goals

Each goal can contain text and up to ten images. Category folders and card stacks are ways the app displays them.

Where to start

Vision board → Add goal

  1. Tap Add goal, choose a category and describe the goal. Add up to ten pictures if you like.
  2. Browse category folders or switch to card stacks. Touch and hold a stack to reorder it.
  3. To change status: open a goal → … → Edit goal. You can also touch and hold a card in a category and select Mark as manifested.

2. Goal-menu affirm: practicing affirmations

If you repeatedly judge yourself harshly, try changing one statement to something you are willing to use.

Affirmations Affirmation

A deliberately chosen statement repeated to practice new self-talk. The term describes a type of statement, not a required number of readings or repetitions.

Origins
Louise Hay is one of the well-known authors associated with modern affirmation practice.
Approach
Hay proposes that choosing positive statements can retrain habitual thinking and speech by repeatedly encountering a self-description you hope to accept.
Effects & evidence
Stronger statements are not necessarily better. Research found that some participants with low self-esteem felt worse after repeating strongly positive self-statements.

Resources: Louise Hay: affirmations · Wood et al. (2009): original study

Search terms: Positive Affirmations；Louise Hay；Positive Self-statements

In FlowerDiary · Goal affirm, 369 writing and Create SUB

Affirm lets you read a statement and confirm it; 369 schedules writing; Create SUB turns statements into audio. They are not synonyms.

Where to start

Vision board → open a goal → top-right … → affirm

  1. Wait for the full statement to appear and read its meaning.
  2. Tap “yes!” three times at your own pace, then continue to the next statement.
  3. Open the statement library at the top right to add, edit or delete sentences. Goals share this library.
  4. Close when finished. Confirmations do not create journal entries or automatically complete goals.

Keep these three names distinct

An affirmation is a kind of statement. Affirm is FlowerDiary’s read-and-confirm practice. 369 is a separate writing tool organized by time periods. Their progress and saving rules differ.

3. Scripting

If you know what you want but cannot picture everyday life with it, try scripting.

Scripting Scripting

Write as the author of your life, describing scenes, dialogue and feelings as if your wish has already come true.

Origins
Esther and Jerry Hicks list Scripting as Process 9 in Ask and It Is Given (2004), a traceable representative source.
Approach
In the Hicks approach, writing helps you experience fulfillment in advance and align your feelings with the life you want.
Effects & evidence
This is an imagination and writing practice. Adding detail does not mean events will unfold exactly as written.

Resources: Book contents and bibliographic details

Search terms: Manifestation Scripting；Ask and It Is Given；Process 9；Esther Jerry Hicks

In FlowerDiary · Scripting

The app groups writing into dawn, morning, afternoon and night. These are prompts; writing just one section is fine.

Where to start

+ → Scripting

  1. Choose dawn, morning, afternoon or night. Use prompts such as what you see or how your body feels to describe a scene.
  2. One section is enough. Use Previous and Next to review the sections.
  3. Tap Save today’s script to create an entry. Saving again that day updates the existing script.
  4. Unsaved content remains a draft organized by chapter and date. A draft is not a journal entry.

4. 369 writing

If free writing is distracting and you prefer a set number of lines, try 369.

369 writing 369 Method

Choose an affirmation about your wish. A common practice is writing it three times in the morning, six at noon and nine in the evening—a schedule for repeated writing.

Origins
Popular in modern manifestation communities. Its original creator has not been reliably verified, and online attributions to Tesla lack reliable sourcing.
Approach
Use fixed repetitions across three parts of the day to keep returning to the same wish.
Effects & evidence
A schedule can support a habit. Claims that the numbers 3, 6 and 9 possess special manifestation powers lack reliable validation.

Resources: Checking the source of the Tesla quote

Search terms: 369 Method；369 Manifestation；369 Journaling

In FlowerDiary · 369 writing

Enter today’s affirmation at the top, then use three morning, six noon and nine evening lines. Complete this period fills blank lines. You still need to save after completing all periods.

Where to start

+ → 369 writing

  1. Fill in Today’s affirmation at the top to choose your statement.
  2. Below are 3 morning, 6 noon and 9 evening input lines. Write each line separately; entering the top statement does not automatically fill them.
  3. Tap Complete this period when done. Only lines still blank are filled from the top statement. The period then becomes read-only.
  4. Once all three periods are complete, Save today’s entry appears. Tap it to save the practice and a journal entry.
  5. Leave before finishing and resume the draft the same day. The app does not lock morning, noon or evening to the clock, or require a particular streak.

Where it is saved

The saved entry appears under Affirmations. This is a journal category; it does not represent an affirm session or add content to the affirm library.

5. Signs and outcomes

When feedback or a coincidence occurs, record what happened before adding your interpretation.

Manifestation signs Manifestation Signs

A community term for events, coincidences or associations connected to a wish, sometimes called “responses from the universe”.

Origins
A community expression without a single professional standard of assessment.
Approach
Record the event and its meaning to you, distinguishing what happened from how you interpret it.
Effects & evidence
A meaningful coincidence is not the same as an achieved outcome. Notice whether you are attending only to information that supports your wish.

Resources: APA: confirmation bias

Search terms: Manifestation Signs；Synchronicity；Confirmation Bias

In FlowerDiary · Signs appeared

Record related experiences. Linking a goal can increase its signs count, but does not automatically mark it manifested.

Where to start

+ → Signs appeared

  1. Write the facts first, then your feelings and next step.
  2. Add photos or select the most relevant linked dimension if needed.
  3. Tap Save. A matching goal gains a signs entry, and an unfinished goal can show Signs appeared.
  4. When the goal is actually achieved, mark it Manifested yourself on the vision board.

6. Limiting beliefs and releasing

When “I cannot do this” keeps returning, record the judgment before exploring the tension it brings.

Limiting beliefs Limiting Belief

In personal growth, fixed judgments that narrow choices or discourage action, such as “If I fail, I must be incapable”.

Origins
A common personal-growth term. It can be understood alongside core beliefs and rigid assumptions in cognitive behavioral theory, but the terms are not identical.
Approach
Describe the situation and judgment, examine the evidence, and explore a more balanced understanding and feasible action.
Effects & evidence
Recording a thought, changing an interpretation and completing a release practice are different actions.

Resources: Beck Institute: cognitive behavioral theory

Search terms: Limiting Beliefs；Core Beliefs；Cognitive Restructuring

In FlowerDiary · Limiting beliefs

Save the specific thought first. In that day’s entry, tap Release. Saving a belief does not automatically complete a release.

Where to start

+ → Limiting beliefs

  1. Write the thought as it appeared and the specific situation that prompted it.
  2. Tap Save. Find this belief in today’s journal afterward.

Letting go / Sedona Method Letting Go / Sedona Method

Letting go broadly means reducing resistance to feelings. The Sedona Method is a particular self-help approach using questions to allow and explore releasing present feelings.

Origins
The Sedona Method traces its origins to Lester Levenson and has been taught and shared by Hale Dwoskin and others.
Approach
Allow the feeling, then use simple questions to explore loosening your hold on it. The aim is less tension and clearer thinking; repeat as needed.
Effects & evidence
Research on emotional acceptance can help explain the approach but does not directly validate a specific Sedona sequence.

Resources: Sedona: official history · Official question examples

Search terms: The Sedona Method；Lester Levenson；Hale Dwoskin；Letting Go

In FlowerDiary · Release practice

The cards draw on Sedona questions, in the order Allow → Would you? → Could you? → When? You can also use your own audio guidance.

Where to start

Journal → Today → today’s belief → Release; or + → Release practice

  1. Release beside a belief brings its text into the guided cards. You can also start Release practice independently through +.
  2. Answer from your experience: can you allow the feeling, would you let it go, could you let it go, and when? Repeat if needed.
  3. Tap Complete release, then OK, done to save. If you started from a belief entry, its text is retained and marked Released.

What Released means

It marks completion of a release practice. If the same thought returns later, you can record it and practice again.

7. SUB and audio creation

If you enjoy listening, turn statements you accept into audio instead of relying only on writing.

SUB / subliminal audio Subliminal Audio

Manifestation communities often use SUB to mean quiet suggestions mixed with background audio. Strictly, subliminal refers to stimulation below the threshold of conscious perception.

Origins
Subliminal is a psychological concept. A community label on an audio track does not mean its stimuli have been experimentally verified as subliminal.
Approach
Listeners hope repeated suggestions can influence beliefs or self-evaluation without demanding attention. A common approach mixes quiet suggestions with background sound.
Effects & evidence
Not hearing speech clearly does not mean its perceptual threshold has been measured. Greenwald et al. (1991) found no claimed memory or self-esteem improvements from the self-help tapes tested in their double-blind study.

Resources: APA: definition of subliminal · Greenwald et al. (1991): double-blind study

Search terms: Subliminal Perception；Subliminal Self-help Audiotapes；Affirmation Audio

In FlowerDiary · Create SUB and Listen to SUB

The app creates affirmation audio with adjustable voice and background levels; it does not measure individual perceptual thresholds. Creating and listening have separate entry points.

Where to start

+ → Create SUB / Listen to SUB

  1. Choose recording or text-to-speech in the creation page. You can also start with the library or a full script.
  2. Preview a recording. For text, tap Generate voice, then choose background sound.
  3. Enter a unique title, choose an album, adjust settings and tap Preview 20 seconds.
  4. Generate when ready. A successful creation is saved to your audio library. Open Listen to SUB to play it.

Check how it sounds first

Use Preview 20 seconds to check voice and background levels before generating the full track. Inaudible speech, more tracks or longer listening do not indicate a stronger effect.

8. Meditation: a few guided quiet minutes

Body and breath awareness for adults. Start with 3 or 5 minutes; stop anytime rather than enduring discomfort.

Mindfulness meditation Mindfulness Meditation

Attend to your body, natural breathing, sounds and thoughts as they arise, returning when you notice your attention has wandered.

Origins
Mindfulness has roots in meditation traditions. Organizations such as the NHS and UCLA introduce modern foundational practices.
Approach
Start in a safe, comfortable position. Notice your body, then choose breathing or points of contact as an anchor. Open your eyes, adjust or stop whenever you need.
Effects & evidence
Public resources describe these principles but do not establish therapeutic effects for this app’s courses. Chakra names come from traditional beliefs, not proven medical mechanisms.

Resources: NHS: mindfulness · UCLA: introductory practices

Search terms: Mindfulness Meditation；Body Scan；Breath Awareness

In FlowerDiary · Meditation

For adults, with original Mandarin guidance lasting 3, 5, 12, 19, 25 or 45 minutes. The 12/19-minute sessions offer body themes. Read the notice before your first session; start with 3 or 5 minutes. Pause or stop anytime—there is no need to endure discomfort.

Where to start

+ → Meditation

  1. On first use and after important notice updates, read Before you practice and choose Understood, continue—or leave. Then choose your meditation. The top area offers a brief introduction, notice, explanations and resources. Use the dropdowns to select duration and available themes, review the timeline and start.
  2. The opening 12 seconds include preparation and a gentle bowl cue. Follow Mandarin guidance for body and natural breath awareness. Continue the preceding practice during quiet intervals. The English interface also uses Mandarin narration.
  3. Pause and End this practice stay at the bottom. Open your eyes, adjust your position or stop if uncomfortable. Opening feedback pauses audio; it does not resume automatically.
  4. After finishing, review feelings and guidance or open User feedback from Meditation or Profile. It provides links to App Store reviews and Xiaohongshu comments or private messages; no journal or device information is attached.
  5. A full session successfully saved creates a usage record, not evidence of health effects. Stopping early requires no make-up session and does not affect other features.

Start with a short session

You do not need a completely empty mind. When distracted, gently return to the breath. Pause or stop if uncomfortable.

9. Book of Answers

Write a question, draw a response and consider what the words bring to your attention.

Book of Answers

Draw a random response from a built-in collection. Save the question, answer and stationery to revisit later.

This is the name of the app’s random-response tool, not a defined manifestation technique.

Where to start

+ → Book of Answers (subscription required)

  1. Write your question and choose paper, pattern and font.
  2. Tap Give it to the Book of Answers, then wait for the letter to unfold.
  3. Save retains the question, answer and page number. Burning the letter leaves this exchange unsaved.
  4. Revisit saved letters in your journal. Saved questions and answers cannot be edited.

Using the response

If you draw “Wait a moment”, ask what information is still missing. The tool does not analyze your question or predict the future. Make decisions using the facts available to you.

10. Recording and reflection

If you have tried several methods but cannot tell what suits you, looking back may be more useful than adding more practice.

Reflection Reflection

Review facts, actions and experiences over a period, then decide what to keep or change.

Origins
A common practice in reflective writing and self-observation, without a single manifestation-school origin.
Approach
Separate what you did, how it felt and what you will do next.
Effects & evidence
Entry counts describe frequency; they cannot establish which method caused an outcome.

Search terms: Reflective Journaling；Reflection；Journal Review

In FlowerDiary · Journal and usage statistics

Today, Calendar and Garden let you revisit content. Usage statistics show daily entry counts across chapters. They provide content and frequency information respectively.

Where to start

Journal → Today / Calendar / Garden

  1. Choose a date in Calendar or a category in Garden and read a few earlier entries.
  2. Today search covers today only. Use Calendar for earlier dates and Garden to search by type.
  3. Garden’s “latest ·” shows the category’s most recent date. Release entries use completion dates.
  4. Choose a few entries to review each week and record your observations as a new entry today. Earlier dates and archived journals are read-only.

These views cover the current chapter. See Reference for earlier chapters, cross-chapter statistics, sharing and syncing.

Separate quantity from what you gain

Counts and streaks describe habits. More useful reflection questions include: what did a practice help me understand, what step did I take, and what will I do next?
